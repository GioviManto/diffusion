\begin{proposition}[Exact gradient from one forward--backward pass]
\label{prop:fisher}
Fix the grid $\gridpt{1},\dots,\gridpt{\ngrid}$ and let $\kernelth$ be the induced finite-state
transition matrix, differentiable in $\params$. With $\Xis$ the expected transition mass,
indexed \textbf{child-first} to match
$[\kernelth]_{kl} = \kernel_\params(\gridpt{k}\mid\gridpt{l})$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:xi}
\Xis_{kl} \;=\; \sum_{\mu=1}^{\nseq}\sum_{i=2}^{\nsites}
P\!\left(a_i=\gridpt{k},\; a_{i-1}=\gridpt{l} \;\middle|\; \vx^\mu\right),
\end{equation}
the gradient of the \emph{discretised} marginal log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fisher}
\nabla_\params \Lik(\params)
= \sum_{\mu}\E_\params\!\left[\nabla_\params \log P_\params(\va,\vx^\mu)\,\middle|\,\vx^\mu\right]
= \big\langle \Xis(\params),\; \nabla_\params \log \kernelth \big\rangle .
\end{equation}
Both $\Xis$ and the evidence come from a single complete forward--backward pass --- one forward and
one backward recursion, not one directional sweep --- so \eqref{eq:fisher} is of the same
asymptotic order as evaluating the likelihood, with no additional message pass and no
backpropagation through the recursion. It is not free: accumulating $\Xis$ over all $(k,l)$
dominates the constant at large $\ngrid$.
\end{proposition}

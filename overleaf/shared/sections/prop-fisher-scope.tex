\begin{remark}[Discrete and continuous are separate claims]
\label{rem:fisher-scope}
Proposition~\ref{prop:fisher} is a statement about the \emph{finite-state model on the grid}. There
the interchange of $\nabla_\params$ with a finite sum is unconditional, and the returned gradient
is exact for the discretised likelihood up to floating point --- which is what the
$10^{-9}$ finite-difference agreement of Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} measures.

The corresponding continuum identity,
$\nabla_\params \Lik = \int\!\!\int \xi_\params(a,a')\,\nabla_\params \log
\kernel_\params(a'\mid a)\,\dd a\,\dd a'$, also holds, but it needs hypotheses the discrete
statement does not: $\kernel_\params(a'\mid a)$ differentiable in $\params$, support not depending
on $\params$, and an integrable dominating function for $\partial_\params \kernel_\params$ so that
differentiation and integration may be exchanged. Our kernels satisfy these --- Gaussian and
Gaussian-mixture innovations are smooth in $\params$ with full support, and Laplace is smooth in
$\corr$ away from the residual's kink --- but the two results should not be conflated, and the
numbers we report certify the discrete one.
\end{remark}

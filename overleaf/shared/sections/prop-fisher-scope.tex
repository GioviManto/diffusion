\begin{remark}[Discrete and continuous are separate claims]
\label{rem:fisher-scope}
Proposition~\ref{prop:fisher} is a statement about the \emph{finite-state model on the grid}. There
the interchange of $\nabla_\params$ with a finite sum is unconditional, and the returned gradient
is exact for the discretised likelihood up to floating point --- which is what the
$10^{-9}$ finite-difference agreement of Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} measures.

The corresponding continuum identity,
$\nabla_\params \Lik = \int\!\!\int \xi_\params(a,a')\,\nabla_\params \log
\kernel_\params(a'\mid a)\,\dd a\,\dd a'$, also holds, but it needs hypotheses the discrete
statement does not: $\kernel_\params(a'\mid a)$ differentiable in $\params$, support not depending
on $\params$, and an integrable dominating function for $\partial_\params \kernel_\params$ so that
differentiation and integration may be exchanged.

Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture innovations satisfy the hypotheses \emph{as written}: both are
smooth in $\params$ with full support. Laplace does not, and saying so matters because Laplace is
the innovation law the reported runs use. Its density $\propto \exp(-|a'-\corr a|/b)$ has a kink
at $a'=\corr a$, so $\partial_\corr \kernel_\corr$ fails to exist on that set and the proposition's
smoothness hypothesis is violated there. The identity survives, but by a different argument: the
kink set is Lebesgue-null, the derivative exists almost everywhere, and the difference quotients
are dominated locally uniformly in $\corr$, so dominated convergence still licenses the exchange.
The discrete statement is untouched either way --- on the grid the kink is a finite set of entries
and the sum is differentiated term by term --- and it is the discrete one the reported numbers
certify.
\end{remark}

\chapter{The Object of Study, and the Minimal Model}
\label{ch:development}\label{sec:development}

Before any of the analysis, two choices have to be justified: that the
quantity worth studying is the \emph{joint} score of a whole trajectory
rather than a per-frame score, and that the stationary Gaussian AR(1) chain
under coordinatewise OU noise is the right model in which to study it. This
short chapter settles both. The exploratory models that led here, and the
sequence of formulations they ruled out, are recorded in the companion
compendium rather than in this document.

\section{Why the joint score}\label{sec:dev-correction}

Standard diffusion models generate \emph{static} objects; the data of
interest here are \emph{dynamic}, a data point being a sequence of frames
indexed by an internal time $u = 0, 1, \dots, K-1$ --- a video, a rotating
shape, a stochastic trajectory. Two times therefore appear from the outset,
and keeping them apart is a permanent discipline of this thesis: the
\emph{internal} time $u$ of the sequence, and the \emph{diffusion} time $t$
of the noising channel, which acts independently on every frame.

It is tempting to treat such a trajectory as $K$ separate samples, computing
each frame's noisy marginal score independently. In the Gaussian model that
carries most of this thesis, that is not merely lossy --- it is empty.

\begin{proposition}[One-frame marginals in the stationary Gaussian
chain]\label{prop:dev-marginal}
Let the clean chain be stationary Gaussian AR(1) with unit marginal variance,
corrupted by the variance-preserving channel
$x = e^{-t} a + \sqrt{1 - e^{-2t}}\, z$, $z \sim \Nd(0,1)$ independent of $a$.
Then every noisy frame is marginally $\Nd(0,1)$ at every diffusion time, the
per-frame marginal score is exactly $-x_k$, and the collection of one-frame
marginals carries zero Fisher information about the coupling $\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This is immediate: variance preservation gives
$\operatorname{Var}(x) = e^{-2t}\operatorname{Var}(a) + (1 - e^{-2t}) = 1$, and a Gaussian $a$ makes $x$
Gaussian, so the marginal law is $\Nd(0,1)$ whatever $\alpha$ is and cannot
depend on it.

\begin{remark}[The same statement is false off the Gaussian
model]\label{rmk:dev-nongaussian}
Variance preservation alone gives only $\operatorname{Var}(x) = 1$; it does not give
Gaussianity, and the distinction matters because the non-Gaussian chains of
Chapters~\ref{ch:laplace}--\ref{ch:em-results} are the ones the rest of this
thesis measures. Writing $\varphi$ for characteristic functions,
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_{x}(u) \;=\; \varphi_a\!\big(e^{-t}u\big)\,
  \exp\!\Big[-\tfrac{1 - e^{-2t}}{2}\,u^2\Big],
  \label{eq:dev-cf}
\end{equation}
so if $x$ were exactly $\Nd(0,1)$ at some finite $t$, dividing by the Gaussian
factor would force
$\varphi_a(e^{-t}u) = \exp[-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-2t}u^2]$ --- that is, $a$ Gaussian.
A genuinely non-Gaussian frame therefore stays non-Gaussian under any finite
amount of Gaussian corruption, and its one-frame score stays nonlinear;
Section~\ref{sec:l-K1} computes that score for a Laplace prior and shows it
approaching $-x$ only as $t \to \infty$.
\end{remark}

\noindent So the correct general statement is narrower than
``per-frame is empty'', and it is about \emph{dependence} rather than about
information as such. One-frame marginals can see the innovation law, and can
see a parameter that changes the marginals; what no collection of them can
recover is how consecutive frames are coupled, since coupling is a property of
the joint law and of no marginal of it. In the Gaussian case those two
statements collapse into one, because the marginals are $\alpha$-free by
Proposition~\ref{prop:dev-marginal}; off it they come apart, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:ring} proves the sharper version for one specific parameter ---
that the rotation rate of the ring model has exactly zero Fisher information in
every one-frame marginal, under that model's rotational symmetry.

A sequence is \emph{one} sample from a joint law, and the quantity that
drives the reverse dynamics is the joint score of the whole noisy trajectory,
the gradient of $\log P_t(x_0, \dots, x_{K-1})$, whose $k$-th component is a
conditional expectation over the \emph{full} posterior of the clean frames,
as in \eqref{eq:tweedie-joint}. That is the object in which the temporal
structure lives. Chapter~\ref{ch:gaussian} exhibits the contrast in closed form
at $K = 2$.

This also settles the shape of the structural question. One might hope that
consecutive frame-blocks of the score are related by a fixed map, a
``propagator'',
\begin{equation}
  \score_{:,u}(x, t) \;\approx\; \mathcal{P}_t\big( \score_{:,u-1}(x, t) \big),
  \label{eq:dev-propagator}
\end{equation}
so that a learning system could hold one block and one map instead of $K$
blocks. What the joint view shows is that the structure worth studying is not
a map between per-frame scores at all, but the coupling pattern of the joint
score --- a statement about a matrix in the Gaussian case, and about a
posterior in general. That is the object the rest of this thesis analyses.

\section{The minimal model}\label{sec:dev-minimal}

Among the candidate dynamic objects --- rotations, coupled phases, bounded
trajectories with reflections, curved data manifolds --- the stationary
Gaussian AR(1) chain under coordinatewise OU noise is the minimal one in
which the joint score is nontrivial yet exactly computable: one coupling
parameter $\alpha$ for the internal dynamics against one noise parameter $t$
for the corruption, linear algebra available at every step, and every
structural question (coupling range, locality, message passing) posable in
closed form.

The requirement doing the work here is \emph{exact computability}. The whole
programme of this thesis is to measure what approximations cost --- Gaussian
message closure in Chapter~\ref{ch:laplace}, an estimated transition kernel
in Chapters~\ref{ch:em-method} and~\ref{ch:em-results}, a finite receptive
field in Chapter~\ref{ch:gaussian} --- and every one of those measurements
needs a reference that is known rather than approximated. A model in which
the answer is available in closed form is not a simplification of the
question; it is what makes the question answerable at all.

The nonlinear models are set aside at that point deliberately, as targets for
after the solvable case is understood. Chapter~\ref{ch:ring} takes up the
first of them.

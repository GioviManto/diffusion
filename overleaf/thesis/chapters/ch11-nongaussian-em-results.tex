\chapter{What Structure Buys, and Where It Stops Paying}
\label{ch:em-results}

Chapter~\ref{ch:em-method} built an estimator that is exact in its inference
step and approximate only in its numerical representation and its
optimisation budget. This chapter asks what that estimator is worth. Four
questions organise it, in the order the evidence answers them most cleanly.

\begin{quote}
How much does the structured estimator reduce the data needed to match a
given denoising accuracy; does that pointwise advantage survive when the
estimated score is used to \emph{generate}, by integrating the reverse
process; how much mixture capacity does the model need; and how far can the
Markov assumption itself be wrong before the structure stops helping?
\end{quote}

Every comparison below fits the mixture kernel of \eqref{eq:em-mixture-kernel}
by the EM procedure of Chapter~\ref{ch:em-method} and compares it against
neural denoisers trained by ordinary denoising score matching on the same
noised data, with no access to the Markov factorisation, the linear kernel
form, or homogeneity across sites --- three structural facts the estimator is
given and the networks are not. Two network architectures recur throughout: a
fully connected MLP with no locality bias, and a local convolutional network
matched in receptive field to the estimator's own locality (Chapter~9's
theme, transported here). The comparison measures the value of the structure
supplied, not a general claim about neural denoisers.

\section{Pointwise data efficiency}\label{sec:em-pointwise}

Under the correctly specified model, at the data budgets tested, the
structured estimator attains lower relative denoising error than the global
MLP by a factor of \textbf{9--14} across seven doublings of the training set,
with no trend in the factor as the budget grows (Figure~\ref{fig:em-sample-eff}).
Against the locality-respecting convolutional network the margin narrows to
\textbf{2--4}, so more than half of the fully connected network's deficit is
attributable to its architecture rather than to the estimator's advantage ---
consistent with the locality results of Chapter~\ref{ch:ring} and the
receptive-field argument of Chapter~\ref{ch:gaussian}.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{fig_sample_efficiency.pdf}
\caption{Relative denoising error against training-set size, mean over six
replicates with standard errors, for the EM/BP estimator and the two network
baselines reported separately. Source:
\texttt{outputs/exp\_07\_em\_vs\_score\_network/}.}
\label{fig:em-sample-eff}
\end{figure}

This comparison was originally selected by an oracle --- the network
parameterisation (predicting the noise or the clean signal) and, in a
separate sweep, the receptive-field radius, were both chosen on the same
test bundle used to report the result. Re-selecting on a disjoint validation
split instead agrees with the test-set choice in 33 of 35 cells for the
parameterisation and 54 of 60 for the two-axis receptive-field sweep; the
mean advantage moves from $11.65\times$ (test-oracle) to $11.66\times$
(validation-selected), and the receptive-field ratio from $3.846$ to
$3.862$. Both oracles were therefore worth less than $1.005\times$ --- a
genuine methodological correction with no measurable effect on the
conclusion.

\section{How much mixture capacity is enough}\label{sec:em-capacity}

Enlarging $C$ in \eqref{eq:em-mixture-kernel} should help until the mixture
can represent the true innovation law and stop helping after. Held-out
log-evidence per edge, fit and scored on disjoint data, rises with $C$ but
\emph{saturates}: paired against $C=1$ on identical training and evaluation
data within each cell (six paired seeds, $N=128$),

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$C$ & paired $\Delta$(evidence/edge) & s.e. & beyond 2 s.e.? & effective $C$ \\
\midrule
2  & $+0.000013$ & $0.000090$ & no  & $2.00$ \\
4  & $+0.001286$ & $0.000107$ & yes & $3.82$ \\
8  & $+0.001735$ & $0.000136$ & yes & $7.70$ \\
16 & $+0.001740$ & $0.000152$ & yes & $15.50$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent$C=2$ buys nothing over a single Gaussian innovation; capacity
begins to pay at $C=4$; and $C=8$ and $C=16$ differ by $5\times10^{-6}$,
inside one standard error, so nothing further is bought past $C\approx8$. The
same pattern holds on the primary pointwise metric --- held-out denoising MSE
against the Bayes denoiser, paired the same way --- where $C=8$ is the paired
optimum at both $N=128$ and $N=512$, $C=16$ is not distinguishable from it,
and at $N=512$ the smallest capacity $C=2$ is measurably \emph{worse} than
$C=1$: extra parameters cost more than they buy below the capacity the data
supports. This is not a resolution artefact --- the narrowest fitted component
stays at least $8.8$ grid spacings wide in every one of these cells.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{fig_capacity.pdf}
\caption{Held-out denoising MSE and generated-sample fidelity against mixture
capacity $C$, three seeds, at a fixed EM budget of 40 iterations. Read
alongside the caveat below: the shape-dependent curves in this figure are
confounded with EM convergence rate; the evidence-based saturation reported
in the text, from a separate paired design, is not. Source:
\texttt{outputs/exp\_16/}.}
\label{fig:em-capacity}
\end{figure}

\begin{remark}[This capacity picture is qualified, and the qualification
matters]\label{rmk:em-capacity-confound}
Every fit contributing to Figure~\ref{fig:em-capacity} and to any curve in
$C$ that depends on the \emph{fitted or generated shape} of the innovation
(as opposed to held-out evidence or runtime) used the fixed EM budget of 40
iterations. Section~\ref{sec:em-convergence} measured that the innovation
shape can need on the order of 2000 iterations to settle --- a factor of
roughly 50 --- and, applied to this specific sweep, found that across 72
seed-configurations \textbf{93\% had not settled in the shape coordinate by
iteration 40}. Because a larger $C$ has more shape parameters to move within
the same fixed budget, under-iteration alone produces a curve that looks
exactly like a capacity effect whether or not capacity is doing anything. A
direct check attributes roughly \textbf{96\% of the apparent capacity effect}
in this configuration to convergence rate at the fixed budget, not to
capacity. The held-out-evidence saturation reported in the main text above
survives this concern, because it is a paired comparison scored by an
average over the whole density that saturates early in the optimisation; the
runtime measurement below is unaffected for the same reason. Anything
resting on fitted or generated \emph{shape} at $C>1$ in the rest of this
section, and in Section~\ref{sec:em-generative} where those same $C=40$-iteration
fits reappear, should be read as evidence for a real phenomenon at an
under-converged operating point, not as a claim about the converged
estimator. Re-running to a convergence criterion rather than a fixed count is
identified as the direct fix and was not completed as part of this thesis.
\end{remark}

Runtime is close to flat across the whole capacity sweep, $13.5\to14.3$
seconds per iteration from $C=2$ to $C=16$ while the number of free
parameters grows $6\to48$, confirming that the E-step's $O(nM^2)$ cost
dominates and only the M-step scales with $C$ --- this measurement, like the
evidence saturation above, is unaffected by
Remark~\ref{rmk:em-capacity-confound}.

\section{Pointwise accuracy does not determine generative fidelity}
\label{sec:em-generative}

The comparisons above are all pointwise: they score a denoiser against a
single noised observation. Using the estimated score to \emph{generate},
by integrating the reverse SDE from noise, is a different test, and the
central finding of this chapter is that the two tests disagree.

The generative metric is the excess kurtosis of the residual
$r_i = x_i - \alpha x_{i-1}$ computed on a generated sample at a small
diffusion time --- an AR-filtered residual, not literally the clean innovation:
$r_i = \alpha\varepsilon_i + \sqrt{\Deltat}(z_i - \alpha z_{i-1})$ has a
predicted, non-zero lag-one autocorrelation of $-0.0997$ at $t=0.02$ (rising
in magnitude with $t$), confirmed by measurement and small on the clean
chain ($|\text{ACF}|<0.02$). With that naming caveat in place, it remains
the sharpest discriminator available between innovation families at matched
covariance, against a closed-form target of $1.9098$ excess kurtosis for the
Laplace-innovation chain used throughout this comparison.

Under a corrected generation protocol --- continuous log-uniform training
noise levels rather than five discrete ones, removing an interpolation
artefact that had been mixed into the earlier comparison --- the two
network-free arms (\texttt{exact} and the EM/BP estimator) are, as they must
be, bit-identical to the previous protocol; the correction is isolated to
the trained networks, both of which \emph{improve} under it. The result
across four training budgets:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrl@{}}
\toprule
$N$ & EM/BP & CNN & MLP \\
\midrule
32   & 0.491 & 0.728 & 14.803\ (2/4 seeds unstable) \\
128  & 0.814 & 1.293 & 1.441 \\
512  & 0.897 & \textbf{1.601} & 0.167 \\
2048 & 0.902 & \textbf{1.572} & \textbf{0.046} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent(target $1.9098$; mean over four seeds per cell except $N=32$).
Pointwise, the EM/BP estimator wins by roughly an order of magnitude at every
budget (Section~\ref{sec:em-pointwise}). Generatively, it never exceeds
$0.90$ at any budget, while the local CNN reaches $1.57$--$1.60$ from
$N=128$ up --- closer to the target than the estimator that dominates it
pointwise, at every budget from 128 onward. The dissociation is present
already at $N=128$, where the small-sample instability of the MLP ($N=32$,
where two of four seeds diverge --- an over-parameterisation artefact of
fitting a $128\times128$ network on 32 chains, not a property of the
protocol) has already resolved.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{fig_pointwise_vs_generative.pdf}
\caption{Pointwise denoising MSE against generated-sample excess kurtosis,
one point per estimator/capacity configuration. The two axes are not a
trade-off curve for a single estimator; the sharper statement is the
within-arm MLP result reported in the text. Source:
\texttt{outputs/exp\_16/gen2\_n*/}.}
\label{fig:em-pointwise-vs-gen}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The sharpest version of the result is within one arm.} At
$N=2048$ the global MLP's four per-seed generated-kurtosis values are
$0.097$, $0.069$, $0.019$, $-0.002$ --- all indistinguishable from a purely
Gaussian generated law --- while its own pointwise error over the same range
of $N$ is monotonically \emph{improving}. A single estimator, trained on
more data by every reasonable measure of fit quality, becomes simultaneously
more accurate pointwise and less faithful generatively. This is a stronger
statement than the three-arm ranking above, because it needs no comparison
between different estimators and no assumption about which one is
correctly specified: it is one estimator, disagreeing with itself across two
notions of correctness as its training data grows. The mechanism proposed ---
that a better $L_2$ fit to the posterior mean is smoother, and a smoother
denoiser drives the reverse SDE toward a more Gaussian generated law --- is a
hypothesis consistent with the direction of the effect and has not been
measured directly in this thesis.

\section{Where the Markov assumption stops paying}\label{sec:em-nonmarkov}

Every result so far assumes the clean chain is exactly first-order Markov.
Two controlled violations were measured against an exact reference (linear
Gaussian algebra for the contaminated model, so the estimator being wrong is
not confused with the reference being wrong): a rank-one global latent
shared across all sites at strength $\beta$, and a long-range coupling at
strength $\gamma$ added on top of the chain.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrrl@{}}
\toprule
violation & strength & CNN/EM-BP & Chow--Liu & reading \\
\midrule
global latent & $\beta=0$     & $11.4\to5.4$      & $-0.0009$ & Markov control \\
global latent & $\beta=0.5$   & $4.1\to3.2$       & $+0.0091$ & degraded, still winning \\
global latent & $\beta=1.0$   & $2.1\to3.1$       & $+0.0128$ & \textbf{never inverts} \\
long-range    & $\gamma=0$    & $25.4\to10.5$     & $+0.0014$ & Markov control \\
long-range    & $\gamma=0.10$ & $0.77\to1.32$     & $+0.0574$ & \textbf{inverts at low $t$} \\
long-range    & $\gamma=0.40$ & $0.47\to1.03$     & $+0.1010$ & \textbf{inverts across most of it} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent The two violations behave qualitatively differently, and the
difference is principled rather than incidental. A global latent shared
across every site makes the score residual \emph{exactly} rank one, by a
Woodbury identity; a chain-structured estimator can absorb a rank-one
correction into its fitted coefficients and does --- the fitted $\alpha$
rises and the fitted innovation kurtosis flattens as $\beta$ grows, exactly
tracking the contamination it is misattributing to the chain. Long-range
coupling is only \emph{approximately} low rank, and a chain family cannot
represent it at all: the advantage is gone by $\gamma\approx0.05$--$0.10$ and
the structured estimator's own relative error reaches $57\%$ at high noise
and the strongest tested coupling. The minimum CNN/EM-BP ratio over the full
noise schedule never falls below $2.08$ for the rank-one violation at any
strength up to $\beta=1.0$ --- where half the marginal variance is a shared
global constant --- and falls to $0.47$ for the long-range one. The Chow--Liu
deviation column corroborates the mechanism independently: the fitted chain
stays close to the best possible Markov approximation of the truth under
rank-one contamination and departs from it under long-range coupling,
exactly where a chain can no longer represent what generated the data. The
same qualitative split holds when the innovation law is Laplace rather than
Gaussian: the rank-one violation still never inverts the advantage, with the
minimum ratio at $\beta=1.0$ equal to $1.45$ against an exact non-Markov
reference.

\begin{remark}
The two extends the lesson of Chapter~\ref{ch:laplace}: there, what broke
under a harder innovation law was the \emph{representability of messages} in
a finite-dimensional family, while the tree, the two-sweep schedule, and the
score--posterior identity survived unconditionally. Here, the further
question is what breaks when the graph is not a tree to begin with. The
answer is not "misspecification degrades the advantage" uniformly --- it is
that a chain-structured estimator tolerates the one violation it can
absorb into its own parameters and fails on the one it structurally cannot
represent, and the two are not interchangeable amounts of "how wrong the
model is": they are different in kind.
\end{remark}

\section{Shape is intrinsically harder to identify than correlation}
\label{sec:em-information}

Section~\ref{sec:em-convergence} found that the innovation shape converges
far slower than the autoregressive coefficient under EM. A direct
information-theoretic measurement gives a structural reason: on a
generalised-Gaussian innovation family where a shape parameter $\beta$
moves the fourth moment at \emph{exactly fixed} variance, the efficient
Fisher information for $\beta$ --- after projecting out the nuisance
coordinates $(\alpha, q)$, since raw diagonal entries alone are misleading
when parameters are correlated --- decays two to three orders of magnitude
faster with diffusion noise than the information for $\alpha$ itself:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
true $\beta$ & info loss, $\alpha$ & info loss, $q$ & info loss, $\beta$ (shape) \\
\midrule
1.0 & $78\times$ & $235\times$ & $\mathbf{8{,}738\times}$ \\
1.5 & $83\times$ & $459\times$ & $\mathbf{64{,}227\times}$ \\
3.0 & $56\times$ & $522\times$ & $\mathbf{68{,}460\times}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent (information loss measured between the smallest and largest
diffusion time tested). This is not merely a restatement of the convergence
finding: it is a property of the noised likelihood itself, independent of
any optimiser, and it explains \emph{why} the shape coordinate is slow ---
there is structurally less information about it in noised data at any fixed
sample size, at every diffusion time past the smallest ones. It is also the
reason the capacity sweep of Section~\ref{sec:em-capacity} is exactly the
kind of measurement where an insufficient iteration budget is dangerous: the
coordinate that is hardest to move is also the one with the least signal per
observation.

\section{Scope not covered here}

The estimator of Chapter~\ref{ch:em-method} was also extended, within the
same research package, to video sequences and to natural-image patches, and
to a wavelet-domain hidden-Markov-tree generalisation of the chain
structure used throughout this thesis. Those extensions are reported in the
package's own notebooks and paper rather than reproduced in this chapter:
the image comparison in particular is, by that project's own account, an
unfinished comparison --- the two kernel families compared were fit with
different, not directly comparable factories --- and reporting a number here
without the caveat that project attaches to it would overstate what is
established.

\section{Summary}

Four results, each qualified by exactly how far it was checked. The
structured estimator reduces the data needed for accurate denoising by
$9$--$14\times$ against an unstructured network and $2$--$4\times$ against a
locality-matched one, and this survives a validation-split correction to
the oracle that originally selected it. That pointwise advantage does not
transfer to generation: the sharpest evidence is a single estimator getting
simultaneously better pointwise and worse generatively as its training data
grows. Mixture capacity beyond roughly eight components buys nothing on
held-out evidence or on paired pointwise MSE, in a measurement that survives
the chapter's central methodological caveat; any capacity conclusion resting
on fitted or generated shape at a fixed, modest EM budget should be treated
as evidence for a real phenomenon at an under-converged operating point
rather than a converged one. And the Markov structure this estimator relies
on is not a single amount of "how much structure": it survives a violation
it can absorb into its own parameters and fails, well before generic
intuition about model misspecification would suggest, on one it structurally
cannot represent.

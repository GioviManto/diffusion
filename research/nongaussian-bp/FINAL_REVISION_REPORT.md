### The result that changed

Pointwise error and generated-tail fidelity both improve monotonically in the mixture size:

| $C$ | MSE vs Bayes denoiser | generated excess kurtosis (target 1.910) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.002281 | −0.034 |
| 4 | 0.000510 | 0.812 |
| 8 | 0.000276 | 1.319 |
| 12 | 0.000249 | 1.363 |
| 16 | **0.000234** | **1.487** |

The two axes do not trade off in the range tested. At $C=16$ the estimator leads the CNN
(0.005161, 1.273) on both simultaneously.

## Statements that remain unsupported, and are labelled as such

1. **Identifiability** is assumed, not proved (Assumption 1).
2. **Receptive-field numbers** used a radius selected on the evaluation set; labelled exploratory
   in the note, the compendium and the advisor Q&A. No validation split exists anywhere.
3. **Three quantities live only in test assertions** — the importance-sampling validation, the
   cross-family closure check, and the baseline-competence figure — so they cannot be recomputed
   from committed data. The note quotes the first with its Monte Carlo tolerance and does not
   quote the third at all. (The boundary-mass diagnostic was a fourth; it is now committed.)
4. **Strict stationarity** has never been run; the claim that sampling the invariant law would
   change nothing is a prediction.
5. **Marginal likelihood and runtime against $C$** are not measured, so the joint capacity
   experiment is complete in three coordinates of five.
6. **Non-Markov robustness** is not measured at all. This is the largest gap in the story.
7. **Locality of the score** is probed only through a local-predictor proxy.

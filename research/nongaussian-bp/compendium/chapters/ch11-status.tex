what is finished, what is running, and what has not been started, because several results here
are recent and one of them changed the framing of the accompanying note.

\section{Pointwise accuracy and generative fidelity can disagree}
\label{sec:dissociation}

\codefile{exp\_16} compares the same estimators \emph{through reverse diffusion} rather than
pointwise. The validation passes first: run with the true kernel, the sampler reproduces the
closed-form target for $P_{t_{\min}}$ to within $1.4\%$ across budgets.

At $\nseq=2048$ on a Laplace chain, the pointwise and distributional rankings \emph{disagree}.
The estimator is an order of magnitude better at denoising and three times better on the
covariance profile, while the convolutional network reproduces the innovation law more
faithfully by both kurtosis and divergence. The fully connected network shows it most sharply:
over the same range its denoising error improves twelvefold while its generated kurtosis
degrades from $0.850$ to $0.206$.

That is the observation. It raises two questions --- whether it is an artefact of this
particular setting, and what causes it --- and \S\ref{sec:resolved} answers both. The reader
who wants the conclusion rather than the sequence can skip there directly; what follows is
worth reading only for how the two candidate explanations were separated.

\begin{pitfall}[title=\textbf{The observation itself is in question}]
A third possibility was not on the list when this was written and now looks like the leading one:
that \codefile{exp\_16}'s \texttt{em\_iters=40} stops EM before the innovation shape has
converged, so the estimator arm is being judged underfitted. Rerunning at 200 iterations moves
the generated excess kurtosis from $0.625$ to $1.464$ at $\ncomp=4$ and from $0.871$ to $1.380$
at $\ncomp=8$, against a convolution at $1.367$ --- which reverses the ordering this section
reports. See the box in \S\ref{sec:resolved} and \codefile{simple/SIMPLE\_RESULTS.md}.
\end{pitfall}

\section{Resolved since: the dissociation is a capacity effect}
\label{sec:resolved}

\begin{pitfall}[title=\textbf{Under correction: this section rests on an unconverged fit}]
\textbf{This section should not be defended as written.} Every run it
rests on used \texttt{em\_iters=40}. EM on this kernel converges on $\rho$ by iteration ${\sim}25$
but not on the innovation \emph{shape}, which is still moving at iteration 100: at
$\rho=0.85$, $\nseq=512$, $\ncomp=8$ the fitted excess kurtosis reads $0.84$ at 30 iterations,
$2.30$ at 120 and $2.29$ at 400. A converged-looking $\rho$ trace is not evidence that the kernel
has converged.

Rerunning this experiment's own configuration with the iteration count as the only variable
(Laplace, composite, $\nseq=512$, grid $401$) gives:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrcc@{}}
\toprule
$\ncomp$ & EM iters & fitted kurtosis & generated kurtosis \\
\midrule
4  & 40  & $1.079$ & $0.625$ \\
4  & 200 & $2.538$ & $1.464$ \\
8  & 40  & $1.621$ & $0.871$ \\
8  & 200 & $2.560$ & $1.380$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
against a convolution at $1.367$ and a target of $1.888$. Enlarging $\ncomp$ from 4 to 8 buys
$0.542$ in fitted kurtosis at 40 iterations and $0.022$ at 200, so the capacity effect this
section reports is almost entirely a difference in convergence \emph{rate} at a fixed iteration
budget. The dissociation reverses with it: below the convolution at 40 iterations, at or above it
at 200.

Claims about parameter recovery and sample efficiency elsewhere in this document are unaffected.
The evidence above is one seed at 800 generated chains and 200 integration steps, which is enough
to withdraw the conclusion but not to replace it; the sweep needs rerunning at convergence. See
\codefile{simple/SIMPLE\_RESULTS.md}.
\end{pitfall}

Four sweeps have since returned, and together they settle what the dissociation is.

\paragraph{It is not an artefact of the setting.} Across the four non-Gaussian families of
\S\ref{sec:matched-covariance} at matched covariance, the convolutional network is closer to
the target innovation kurtosis than the estimator at $C=4$ in every case. It persists at
$\nsites\in\{32,64,128\}$. And it is \emph{identical to three decimals} under grid refinement
$\ngrid\in\{401,801,1601\}$ --- the strongest of the three controls, since it removes
quadrature as an explanation outright.

\paragraph{It is a limit of the model class, not of the data.} Two explanations were
available and they differ in consequence. Both the marginal likelihood and the squared-error
loss are bulk-dominated, so the tail --- little mass, high leverage on shape --- is weakly
constrained by either; that is a capacity limit and is fixable. Separately, the channel
destroys innovation-shape information $112$ to $1222$ times faster than correlation information
(\S\ref{ch:estimation}), so the data might simply not carry it; that is not fixable.

\begin{intuition}
The reading that survives is methodological rather than about which method wins. An estimator
can be an order of magnitude better at the task it was fitted for and still generate worse
samples, because the objective it optimises and the statistic the generated distribution
exposes weight the distribution differently. A structured method therefore has to be judged on
what it generates, which is exactly the objection that prompted this experiment.
\end{intuition}

\section{The capacity question, resolved on both axes}
\label{sec:capacity-resolved}

The previous version of this chapter left one question open: the component sweep varied
$\ncomp$ for generation only, so it was possible that buying generative fidelity cost pointwise
accuracy and the two axes traded against each other. That run has returned (SLURM job
\texttt{617485\_[0-4]}, outputs \codefile{outputs/exp\_16/cpoint\_C*/pointwise.csv}), and the
answer is that they do not.

\noindent
(Laplace chain, $\nseq=2048$; pointwise column is the median over five noise levels and three

arms do not depend on $\ncomp$ and are constant down both blocks, which is an internal

Pointwise error falls by a factor of $9.7$ from $\ncomp=2$ to $\ncomp=16$ while generated
kurtosis climbs from $-0.034$ to $1.487$. There is no capacity in the tested range at which the
two axes trade. At $\ncomp=16$ the estimator is ahead of the convolution on both simultaneously:
$0.000234$ against $0.005161$ pointwise, and a kurtosis deficit of $0.434$ against $0.648$.

What this settles and what it does not: it settles that the disagreement observed at $\ncomp=4$
is a property of a too-small innovation model rather than of the information surviving the
channel, since enlarging the model removes it without costing anything on the other axis. It
does not settle that information is never the binding constraint --- the kurtosis curve has not
saturated at $\ncomp=16$, and how much further it can climb is not measured. Nor does the sweep
cover marginal likelihood or runtime against $\ncomp$, which are the two coordinates of the
joint experiment still missing.

\section{Still in progress}
\label{sec:pending}

Nothing above relies on any of these.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Marginal likelihood and runtime against $\ncomp$.} The capacity sweep covers
pointwise error and generated statistics. Adding the likelihood would show whether the
improvement is visible in the objective the estimator actually maximises; adding runtime would
price it.
\item \textbf{A strictly stationary rerun.} Every chain here starts from
$a_1\sim\normal{0}{1}$, which is not the invariant law for non-Gaussian innovations
(Remark~\ref{rem:cov-stationary}). Sampling the invariant law needs a fixed-point computation
per family and has not been done, so the claim that it changes nothing is a prediction.
\item \textbf{Receptive field with a validation split.} See below.
\end{itemize}

\section{Known deficiencies in the current measurements}

Recorded rather than deferred, because each affects how a number should be read.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Error bars: corrected.} An earlier version of this document stated that seeds
varied only the initialisation. That was wrong, and checking rather than assuming is what
caught it: the replicate index is mixed into every seed that draws training data, so each
replicate uses a fresh training set as well as a fresh initialisation. The held-out test set
and its exact reference are deliberately common to all replicates. The intervals therefore
cover both sampling and optimisation variance, which is the protocol one wants.
\item \textbf{One hyperparameter was selected on the evaluation set.} The convolutional
network's receptive field in \codefile{exp\_12} was chosen by an oracle over the same held-out
set the number is reported on. The bias favours the \emph{baseline}, so the quoted margin is
conservative, but the protocol is improper and the run should be repeated with a validation
split.
\item \textbf{Three quantities are asserted only in tests.} The importance-sampling validation,
the cross-family closure check and the baseline-competence figure are checked by the test suite
but never written to an output file, so they cannot be independently recomputed from committed
data. They are marked as such wherever they appear. The boundary-mass diagnostic was in this
list; it is now computed and committed
(\codefile{outputs/exp\_18/boundary.csv}, \S\ref{sec:truncation}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Not started}

\begin{itemize}
\item A hybrid estimator combining a learned chain kernel with a learned low-rank correction,
which is the natural next step for relaxing the Markov assumption.
\item Distillation of the fitted inference-based denoiser into a network, which is the obvious
remedy for its three-hundred-fold inference cost.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
This list until 2026-08-12 also carried the entry ``Anything about non-Markov priors. The
misspecification limitation of \S\ref{ch:markov} is stated and not addressed.'' That was the
project's largest acknowledged gap, and it has been measured: \codefile{exp\_21} covers fifteen
cells --- rank-one contamination $\beta$ and long-range coupling $\gamma$, Gaussian and Laplace
innovations --- and the result is that the structural prior survives rank-one contamination up
to $\beta = 1.0$, where half the marginal variance is a shared constant, and fails under
long-range coupling by $\gamma \approx 0.10$. The entry is removed rather than struck through
because leaving ``not started'' next to a completed sweep is how a reader concludes the sweep
does not exist.
